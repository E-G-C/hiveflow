**ACME Corp – Q4 2025 Earnings Call Executive Summary**
*Board Presentation – 300‑500 words*

**Headline Results**
ACME reported Q4 2025 revenue of **$2.47 billion**, a **+18 %** year‑over‑year increase, and a full‑year 2025 revenue of **$9.2 billion** (+22 % YoY). Gross margin expanded to **71.4 %**, up 310 basis points from the prior year, while non‑GAAP operating margin reached **19.4 %** (up from 14.8 % in Q4 2024). Operating expenses fell to 52 % of revenue, and R&D investment was **$520 million** (21 % of revenue). Free cash flow for the quarter was **$410 million**, with a full‑year FCF of **$1.6 billion**.

**Segment Highlights**

- **Cloud Services**: Generated **$1.1 billion** in revenue, a **+34 %** YoY increase. The segment’s ARR surpassed **$4 billion**, and customer retention remained strong at **97 %**. Net new enterprise customers added 847, bringing total cloud customers to 12,400.

- **Enterprise Software**: Revenue of **$980 million** (+11 % YoY). The AI‑powered analytics suite drove **+58 %** growth, while license revenue stayed flat. Subscription revenue grew **+28 %**, and the subscription mix is expected to reach **75 %** of enterprise software revenue by the end of 2026 (currently 60 %).

- **Professional Services**: Revenue of **$390 million** (+5 % YoY) with a margin of **22 %**, up from 18 %. The segment’s contribution to total revenue remains modest but stable.

**Outlook & Guidance**

- **Q1 2026**: Revenue forecast **$2.55–$2.60 billion** (+19–21 % YoY), operating margin around **20 %**, non‑GAAP EPS **$1.42–$1.48**.     
- **Full‑Year 2026**: Revenue projected **$10.8–$11.2 billion** (+17–22 % YoY), operating margin **21 %**.
- AI investments of **$2 billion** across 2025‑2026 are expected to yield AI‑related revenue of **$2 billion** by 2027, roughly **15–18 %** of total revenue.

**Key Risks & Concerns**

1. **Competitive Pricing Pressure** – While ACME currently enjoys a **62 %** win‑rate against top competitors, the market’s pricing dynamics could erode margins if competitors intensify cost‑based competition.
2. **Subscription Transition Impact** – The shift to a subscription model in enterprise software temporarily dampens reported growth but is intended to improve revenue quality. The transition may delay short‑term revenue growth and requires continued investment in customer acquisition and retention.
3. **AI Investment Return** – The **$2 billion** AI R&D commitment carries execution risk; delays or lower adoption of AI‑powered products could affect the projected **$2 billion** AI revenue by 2027.
4. **Operating Expense Management** – While operating expenses have decreased as a percentage of revenue, continued growth in R&D and professional services could pressure margins if not managed effectively.

**Conclusion**
ACME delivered robust Q4 and full‑year 2025 results, driven by strong cloud growth and a successful transition to subscription in enterprise software. The company’s strategic focus on AI and high‑margin cloud services positions it for continued growth and improved profitability in 2026 and beyond. Board attention should be directed toward monitoring competitive pricing dynamics, the subscription transition’s impact on growth, and the execution of the AI investment strategy.
