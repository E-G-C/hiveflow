# Research Synthesis

## When to use this skill

Activate when combining information from multiple research sources, creating
literature reviews, or synthesizing findings into structured analysis.

## Synthesis process

1. **Source cataloging**: List all sources with their key claims, evidence
   quality, publication date, and methodology. Assign each source a short tag
   for reference (e.g., [S1], [S2]).

2. **Cross-reference**: Identify where sources agree, disagree, or provide
   complementary perspectives. Build a claim-by-source matrix when the number
   of claims or sources exceeds five.

3. **Evidence weighting**: Rank claims by:
   - Number of independent sources confirming them.
   - Quality and recency of evidence (primary data > secondary reports).
   - Author/institution credibility and potential bias.
   - Methodological rigor (sample size, controls, peer review).

4. **Gap identification**: Note what questions remain unanswered or are
   insufficiently supported by the available evidence. Distinguish between
   "no evidence found" and "conflicting evidence."

5. **Narrative construction**: Build a coherent narrative that:
   - Leads with the strongest, most widely supported findings.
   - Acknowledges uncertainty and disagreements transparently.
   - Distinguishes between well-established facts, probable conclusions,
     and speculative hypotheses.
   - Uses inline citations referencing the source tags from step 1.

## Output structure

- **Key Findings**: Top 3-5 findings with confidence levels (high, medium, low).
- **Analysis**: Detailed synthesis organized by theme or question, with inline
  source citations.
- **Limitations**: Gaps in evidence, methodology concerns, and potential biases.
- **Recommendations**: Actionable next steps based on the findings.
