# Document Writing

## When to use this skill

Activate when creating formal documents such as reports, proposals, executive
briefs, whitepapers, or technical documentation.

## Writing process

1. **Audience and purpose**: Identify who will read the document and what
   decision or action it should inform. Adapt vocabulary, depth, and tone
   accordingly (technical audience vs. executive summary, internal vs. external).

2. **Outline first**: Create a hierarchical outline with:
   - Section headings reflecting the logical flow of the argument or narrative.
   - Key points and evidence for each section.
   - Approximate word allocation per section, proportional to importance.

3. **Draft sections**: Write each section following the outline:
   - Lead with the most important information (inverted pyramid).
   - Support claims with evidence, data, and specific examples.
   - Use transitions between sections to maintain reader flow.
   - Keep paragraphs focused — one idea per paragraph.
   - Vary sentence structure; avoid repeating the same pattern.

4. **Revision pass**: Review the complete draft for:
   - Logical consistency across sections — do later sections contradict or
     repeat earlier ones?
   - Redundancy elimination — remove duplicated information.
   - Tone consistency — maintain the same register throughout.
   - Completeness — verify all requirements from the original brief are met.

## Style guidelines

- Use active voice by default; passive voice only when the actor is unknown
  or intentionally de-emphasized.
- Define technical terms on first use.
- Keep sentences under 25 words on average.
- Use bullet or numbered lists for three or more parallel items.
- End each section with a substantive point, not a summary restatement.
- Prefer concrete, specific language over vague generalities.
- Use headings liberally to aid scanning — no section should exceed 500 words
  without a sub-heading.
